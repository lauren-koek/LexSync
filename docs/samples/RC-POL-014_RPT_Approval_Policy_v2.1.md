# Related Party Transaction (RPT) Approval Policy

**Document ref:** RC-POL-014  **Version:** v2.1  **Redline from:** v2.0 (14 Mar 2018)
**Owner:** Group Compliance  **Approved by:** Board of Directors  **Effective date:** [to be confirmed on approval]
**Review cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This policy sets out the framework for identifying, assessing, approving, monitoring and
reporting related party transactions ("RPTs") entered into by the Company and its subsidiaries.

1.2 The policy applies to all directors, officers and employees, and to every transaction,
arrangement or exposure between the Company (or any member of the Group) and a related party,
regardless of value, form or whether consideration is exchanged.

1.3 This policy is to be read together with MAS Notice 643 (Transactions with Related Parties),
the Companies Act, and the Company's Conflicts of Interest Policy (RC-POL-009).

## 2. Board Approval Threshold

A related party transaction exceeding the materiality threshold set under this policy, or any
write-off of exposure to a related party, requires the approval of **a special majority of
three-fourths of the Board, determined based on the total number of directors on the Board,
excluding any director required to abstain due to an interest in the transaction.**

> **Redline note (from v2.0):** The previous requirement — "a simple majority of the Board
> (i.e. more than half of directors present and voting)" — has been replaced by the
> three-fourths special majority above.

*Basis: MAS Notice 643, paragraph 26.*

## 3. Definitions and Materiality Threshold

3.1 **Related party** has the meaning given in MAS Notice 643 and includes directors,
substantial shareholders, key management personnel, and their close relatives and controlled
entities.

3.2 **Materiality threshold** means a transaction, or series of related transactions within any
rolling 12-month period, whose aggregate value equals or exceeds the lower of:
(a) SGD 1,000,000; or
(b) 5% of the Company's latest audited net tangible assets.

3.3 The Board Risk Committee reviews the materiality threshold annually and may recommend
adjustments to the Board.

## 4. Identification and Assessment

4.1 Any employee who becomes aware of a proposed or existing RPT must notify Compliance before
the transaction is committed.

4.2 Compliance maintains a register of related parties and screens proposed transactions against
it. An interested director must declare the nature and extent of their interest at the earliest
opportunity and must not participate in the deliberation or vote on that transaction.

4.3 Each RPT is assessed for whether it is on normal commercial terms and is not prejudicial to
the interests of the Company and its minority shareholders.

## 5. Reporting of Exceptions  *(NEW)*

**Every related party transaction that is an exception to, or does not comply with, this policy
must be reported to the Board on a quarterly basis.**

*Basis: MAS Notice 643, paragraph 18. No equivalent clause previously existed in this policy.*

## 6. Record Keeping

6.1 Compliance retains full records of each RPT — including the approval trail, declarations of
interest, and the terms of the transaction — for a minimum of seven years.

6.2 Records are made available to the internal and external auditors and to MAS on request.

## 7. Roles and Responsibilities

- **Board of Directors** — approves RPTs above the materiality threshold and reviews the
  quarterly exceptions report.
- **Board Risk Committee** — reviews the materiality threshold and oversees RPT monitoring.
- **Group Compliance** — maintains the related-party register, screens transactions, and prepares
  the quarterly exceptions report.
- **Business units** — identify and escalate potential RPTs before commitment.
